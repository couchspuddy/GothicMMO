# Character & Enemy Art Direction
**Vigil — Character Art Doctrine**
*Status: Silhouette and form language locked. Surface/texture treatment inherits from existing Humanity Spectrum and Shaped color doctrine (see Tone & Sensory Bible).*

---

## Purpose and Method

This document does what Environment Art Direction's Layer 2 (Spatial Logic) did for spaces: establishes silhouette and form as a legible information layer, distinct from and prior to color, texture, or any UI element. It builds on the Tone & Sensory Bible's Humanity Spectrum and Shaped color signatures rather than replacing them — those documents establish *what* an Accursed's transformation means; this document establishes *how the body communicates it before anything else does.*

**The governing requirement, stated plainly:** a player must be able to identify an enemy's approximate tier and, where applicable, Shaped archetype from silhouette alone — before a health bar, name plate, or any other UI renders, and before they're close enough to see color or texture detail. This is not an aesthetic preference; it is the same design philosophy already proven by the vital point shimmer and The Read: **information earned by looking, not handed to the player by UI.**

---

## Why Silhouette, Not Just Color, Matters

Color signatures (Feral's green-tan, Predatory's red-silver, Assembled's yellow-brown, Wraith's grey-white) answer "what kind of danger is this" at close-to-medium range. But at longer range, in poor light, or in a fast-moving encounter, color reads slower than silhouette does. A player needs a first-pass read from pure shape and posture, with color serving as *confirmation* once they're close enough, not as the only signal available. This two-layer system (silhouette first, color second) mirrors the same "coarse signal, then precise signal" structure already used in the UI/UX Doctrine's health bar (shape/position for instant reading, color for precise state).

## Silhouette by Tier — Posture as Control

**Thrall.** Human posture, but subtly wrong — not broken, not hunched, simply uncommitted. A stance that doesn't fully commit to any clear intention; movement that starts and doesn't finish. This is the silhouette equivalent of "fights like forgetting" — the body reads as belonging to someone who doesn't know what they're about to do next, because they genuinely don't.

**Retained.** More deliberate and composed than Thrall — this is intentionally counterintuitive and worth stating explicitly. Since Retained "fights like collectors," cold and assessing, the silhouette should read as *more* controlled than Thrall's, not less: balanced weight, a watching stance. The wrongness is not postural collapse — it's that the posture is too controlled for what is still, underneath, a suffering and degraded person.

**Lucid.** Full, correct human posture. Nothing broken, nothing askew, nothing "monstrous" about the silhouette at all. This is the tier where the design temptation to make the silhouette read as threatening must be deliberately resisted — per the Humanity Spectrum, Lucid's horror is *invisibility*, and silhouette is the layer most likely to accidentally undercut that principle by reaching for a "scarier" shape. A Lucid should be indistinguishable from an ordinary person at the silhouette level, full stop, until proximity reveals the eyes or the voice.

**Hollow.** Silhouette breaks entirely, consistent with the scale-discontinuity rule already locked in the Environment Art Direction doctrine. Not humanoid-with-distortion — genuinely uncertain shape, potentially unstable or unclear even as a silhouette. Reserved exclusively for Hollow-tier content.

## Silhouette by Shaped Archetype — Posture as First Tell

Each Shaped archetype's silhouette should be identifiable before its color signature is visible, extending the same inversion logic already established for color into posture and form.

**Feral.** Hunched, weight carried forward, asymmetric stance — closer to quadrupedal readiness than upright standing. The loss of containment (per the archetype's core inversion) should be visible in how the body holds itself, independent of any fur or texture detail.

**Predatory.** Elegant, composed, almost performative posture — upright, graceful, deliberately the *opposite* silhouette instinct from Feral. Because the inversion is loss of generosity into pure taking, the silhouette should read as someone who still carries themselves beautifully, which makes the wrongness colder rather than more visibly monstrous.

**Design decision, stated explicitly:** a Predatory reading as non-threatening or even attractive at range is intentional, not a risk to be designed away. This is consistent with the archetype's nature — someone who still draws people in the way they did in life, except the reaching now means consuming. The correction for potential player confusion is not to soften the silhouette toward genre-conventional monstrousness; it is to ensure color and, once mechanically established, gameplay tells close the gap quickly enough on approach that the moment of recognition reads as unsettling rather than as an unfair surprise.

**Wraith.** Minimal silhouette presence — slight, still, easy to overlook at a glance, consistent with "emptied, not damaged" rather than visibly corrupted. Likely the hardest of the four archetypes to read at range by design; the correct player experience is initially mistaking stillness for absence, then learning to recognize that specific stillness as a threat signal.

**Assembled.** Mass that does not resolve correctly — too many points of articulation, bulk distributed in wrong places, an outline suggesting more physical presence than one body should have. This is the one archetype where silhouette alone should likely be sufficient for identification even at range, appropriate given Assembled's defining nature is the failure to successfully hide (see Tone & Sensory Bible, Assembled resolution) — the wrongness of the mass is the whole point, and it should never read as subtle.

## Design Tenet Alignment

- **Respect the Player's Intelligence** — silhouette-first identification rewards observant players with genuine, actionable information before any UI confirms it, the same philosophy already proven in the vital point and Steadfast systems.
- **The Accursed are never monsters** — the Lucid and Predatory silhouette decisions both actively resist the instinct to make Accursed read as conventionally monstrous, in favor of readings (unsettling normalcy, unsettling elegance) that are more specific and more consistent with who these people were.
- **Diminishment, Not Accumulation** (Tone & Sensory Bible) — every silhouette direction here is described as a loss of control, containment, or presence, never as an addition of monstrous features. Posture communicates what was taken, not what was added.
