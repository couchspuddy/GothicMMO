## The Kindle (Reference — Already Locked Elsewhere)

The Kindle is the game's core social unit — a group of Antecedents operating together, functionally the 4-person party. Already referenced throughout encounter design (Eagle's Landing, party formation doctrine) and locked as terminology in the Narrative Bible. Not redefined here; included for reference since Holds are built as a structure sitting above it.

---

## Why "Hold" Was Chosen Deliberately, Not as a Guild Reskin

Before designing mechanics, worth stating the naming decision explicitly, since it governs everything downstream: a Hold is not simply Vigil's word for "guild." "Hold" already carries real weight in the world's vocabulary — walled settlements, defended territory, the Warden's entire identity built around holding a line. A generic social-tag-and-roster system wearing this name would waste the word's meaning and would be the same category of unexamined genre-default as the resource bars caught earlier in production. A Hold should earn its name by being tied to something communities actually *hold* — shared identity and, ideally over time, shared stakes — rather than functioning as pure social administration.

## Baseline Structure — Locked

**Size:** A Hold is sized as **8 Kindles (32 people)**, not an arbitrary guild-scale number. This is a deliberate mathematical choice, not a hard cap enforced rigidly — the point is that every social structure in the game, from Kindle to Hold, stays coherent multiples of 4, matching the same unit every encounter and party-formation system is already built around. This means Hold-scale content (if ever built) can be designed using the same underlying design language as 4-person content, just scaled by a known, consistent factor, rather than requiring an entirely separate design vocabulary for "large group content."

**Function — transactional, not purely social.** A Hold provides its members with small, largely inconsequential but relevant buffs. Deliberately modest in scope — this is closer to a light communal benefit than a power-defining system, consistent with "Earn Everything" (Hold membership should feel like a nice belonging-bonus, not a required power source that makes solo or small-Hold play feel punished).

**Formation and administration** — not yet specified in detail (roster management, leadership structure, recruitment tools). This is standard MMO social-system territory and can follow reasonably conventional patterns without needing the same level of doctrine-checking as combat-facing systems, since it's administrative rather than experiential.

## Filed Future Concept — Hold-Scale Unlock Content (32-Person Activity)

**Status: explicitly not a current design commitment. A genuine idea worth exploring later, filed alongside Ancients and the Cathedral of Chains raid concepts as content to develop if and when time allows — not before more foundational work is proven.**

### The Concept

A final layer of raid-adjacent content gated behind Hold-scale participation (32 players) rather than standard Kindle-scale (4) or typical raid-scale (likely a small multiple of Kindles, size TBD once raid design begins in earnest). Participation would not require players to belong to the same Hold — two Holds could combine forces (e.g. one contributing 12 players, another 20) to reach the required threshold and attempt the encounter together.

### Why This Was Filed Rather Than Designed Now — Documented Reasoning

This idea was deliberately not developed further in this session, for reasons worth recording so the reasoning isn't lost if revisited later:

**Scale gap.** Every encounter designed to date (Eagle's Landing, the mini-boss, the Bestial Lucid) assumes 1-4 players in tight, readable combat space. 32-person coordinated content is not "the same design bigger" — it is a genuinely different design problem: visual noise management, whether systems built for small-group intimacy (the vital point system's readability, Steadfast's strictly-per-player design, the Selah moment's shared-pause framing) remain meaningful or coherent at 8x scale, and how a shared moment like Selah functions when the "party" may span two Holds who have never played together.

**Flexible vs. fixed population risk.** If the encounter requires dynamic scaling to accommodate variable group sizes (e.g. the 12-and-20 combination example), that is a substantially harder and more failure-prone design and engineering problem than anything else currently in the project — dynamic difficulty scaling across a wide population range is notoriously difficult to make feel fair and interesting at every point on that range, not just at the extremes. If it requires a fixed, exact population instead, it creates a real coordination burden (two specific Holds agreeing to combine, at a specific time, with exactly enough people) that may be difficult to reliably satisfy, particularly during a live game's early population growth.

**Dependency chain.** This concept depends on Hold implementation (not yet built), raid design (deferred), and combat-at-scale validation (untested, since the vertical slice itself hasn't been played by anyone but the designer yet). Designing this now would mean building for content, systems, and population scales that don't yet exist and haven't been proven — the same risk pattern flagged in the earlier discussion about solo-developer scope discipline and the general tendency for high-ambition side-features to quietly consume disproportionate design time relative to their priority.

### What's Worth Preserving From This Conversation

- The 8-Kindle Hold sizing already supports this concept cleanly if it's ever built — no rework needed there.
- The specific framing of asymmetric Hold combination (12 + 20 = 32) is worth revisiting as-is if this is picked back up, since it's a genuinely interesting coordination mechanic distinct from a standard fixed-roster raid.
- This should not be prioritized ahead of: raid design generally, Hold baseline implementation, or validated playtesting of the 4-person combat systems this would need to scale from.

## Design Tenet Alignment

- **Earn Everything** — Hold buffs are deliberately modest, avoiding a structure where Hold membership becomes a required power source rather than a community belonging bonus.
- **The world existed before you arrived** — tying Hold naming and identity to "holding" something (even conceptually, before any literal claimed-territory system exists) keeps the social layer consistent with the game's broader relationship between community and defended space, rather than being a bolted-on generic guild feature.
