## Purpose and Method

Same method as every other doctrine document: derive from what's already locked rather than choosing references first. Several Tone & Sensory Bible pairs already implicitly governed music before this document existed — this document makes those implications explicit and adds the structural framework needed to actually compose against.

## Governing Constraints, Inherited From the Tone & Sensory Bible

**Significant, Not Celebrated.** No triumphant crescendos, swells, or stings on player accomplishment. The world exhales, it does not cheer. This rules out the genre-standard "kill sting," "level-up fanfare," and "boss defeat crescendo" as defaults — any musical acknowledgment of player success should read as release, not celebration.

**Reverent, Not Sacred.** No choir-and-organ, no religious grandiosity, despite the world containing literal cathedral spaces. The Cathedral of Chains should likely sound *emptied of* the grandiosity a player expects from that architecture, not full of it — the absence of expected sacred music is more unsettling than its presence would be.

**Earned Quiet, Not Ambient Calm.** The Selah moment's silence must remain the only true silence in the game. This means the score itself should essentially never go fully silent or fully still outside of Selah — if quiet, contemplative musical passages are scattered throughout regular play, Selah's silence loses its singularity. The score needs its own version of "never fully rests," parallel to the sound design rule it was originally derived from.

**Haunting, Not Frightening.** No jump-scare-style stingers or sudden dissonant stabs as a primary tool. Dread should be sustained and texture-based, not delivered through shock.

---

## The Core Structural Decision: Score Reacts to Place and Structure, Not to Combat State

Two conventional options were considered and rejected in favor of a third:

**Rejected — fully combat-reactive/adaptive scoring** (music intensifies the moment combat starts, layers add during phase changes). Standard genre practice, but risks contradicting "the world doesn't celebrate you" — aggressive combat-responsive scoring is inherently a "the world reacts to your greatness" structure.

**Rejected — fully combat-indifferent, single continuous atmospheric score** (same texture whether walking or fighting, changing only for major narrative beats). Interesting and distinctive, but risks feeling under-differentiated and may not give staged content the escalating tension its encounter design already builds toward.

**Adopted — the score reacts to place and narrative structure, not to combat as a binary event.** This preserves "the world doesn't celebrate you" (individual combat encounters never independently trigger a musical response) while still allowing genuine musical evolution where it's structurally earned — through progression through a defined space, not through the act of fighting itself.

## Score Behavior By Content Type

### Hearth
Warm, major-key, subtle, present in the background rather than foregrounded. **Likely the only sustained major-key musical cue in the entire game** — this is deliberate, and its rarity is what gives it weight. Ties directly to the Environment Art Direction's warm/cold doctrine: Hearth is the one location with a confirmed warm light source, and it should be the one location with a confirmed warm key center.

### Open World
One score per zone, looped, **entirely combat-indifferent.** Combat can occur at any moment in open world content, but the music does not react to it — sound design (a separate, not-yet-built doctrine layer) carries all combat-moment audio texture instead. This reinforces the deliberately lower narrative stakes already locked for open world in the Death & Failure States doctrine ("the world's hostility is slightly easier internally") — nothing in open world combat is urgent enough to change the song.

### Staged Content (Contracts — e.g. Eagle's Landing)
- A **base score** establishes the zone's unease/tone at rest.
- The score **evolves per-encounter** as "things are happening" — escalating urgency during active combat within a structured encounter.
- The score **resets to the base theme between encounters**, rather than remaining elevated.
- **The boss encounter (the den) gets its own wholly distinct score**, separate from the base zone theme and its evolved states.

**Why the reset-between-encounters detail matters:** this creates a musical rhythm — tension, release, reset, repeat — that runs in parallel to the Selah collection rhythm already locked (encounter → intensity → clear → Selah pause → reset). The two systems reinforce each other rather than operating independently, strengthening both.

### Raid Content
Not yet designed — should follow the same content-type-driven logic as staged content once raid design begins in earnest, per the Death & Failure States doctrine's existing raid deferral.

## Open Items — Deliberately Deferred to Post-Playtest

**Per-encounter granularity within staged content.** Whether each individual encounter within a Contract (e.g. Eagle's Landing's three encounters) needs its own distinct evolved musical state, or whether a single "urgency" evolution state serves adequately across all encounters regardless of which one is active, is explicitly left open. This is a feel question that should be answered by testing the vertical slice's actual combat pacing, not resolved through further abstract design discussion. Worth particular attention once tested: whether Encounter 3's structurally unique interrupted-Selah beat (see Eagle's Landing Encounters doc) warrants its own distinct musical treatment, given it already deliberately breaks the rhythm the first two encounters establish.

**Sound design doctrine** (distinct from music) — referenced above as carrying open world's entire combat-moment audio load, but not yet built as its own document. Music and sound design are related but separate disciplines and should eventually get their own full treatment the way environment art received both structural (Layers 1-2) and surface (Layer 3) passes.

**Boss den score specifics** — confirmed as needing to be wholly distinct from the base zone score, but no specific direction (instrumentation, tempo, key) has been designed yet for the Bestial Lucid's den specifically.

**Raid musical structure** — deferred alongside raid design generally.

---

## Design Tenet Alignment

- **Every system serves the feeling** — the reset-between-encounters rhythm was identified as running parallel to the Selah collection rhythm, meaning music now reinforces an already-locked emotional structure rather than operating as an independent, uncoordinated layer.
- **Weight Before Spectacle** — ruling out combat-reactive stings and triumphant cues by default keeps the score in service of atmosphere rather than moment-to-moment player validation.
